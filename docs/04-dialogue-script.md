# RUMIÑAHUI — Dialogue Script
### Prologue (M0.1–M0.2) and Ending (M5.5–M5.7)

Companion document to the Story Bible, Mission List, and Combat Design Doc. Screenplay format, with gameplay direction notes in *[brackets]* where the mission calls for player input rather than pure cutscene.

---

# M0.1 — "NIGHT RAID"

**EXT. PÍLLARO VILLAGE — NIGHT**

A small highland settlement at the forest's edge. Terraced fields above, cook-fires below. PILLAHUASO, 10, sits at the edge of a fire, half-listening to his mother, INTI CHASKA (a local princess, not the same character as adult Chaska), speaking with an elder about the harvest. His older cousin/foster-brother-figure, ANTA (20s, a village warrior who has effectively raised him alongside his mother), sharpens a blade nearby.

> **ANTA:** *(to Pillahuaso, not looking up)* You're staring at the fire like it owes you something.

> **PILLAHUASO:** I'm not staring at anything.

> **ANTA:** *(a small smile)* Good. Then go find more wood before your mother notices you haven't.

*[Gameplay: brief free-roam of the village — light interactions with 2–3 NPCs, establishing the community before it's taken away. No combat.]*

*Pillahuaso crosses the village. A dog barks — wrong pitch, wrong urgency. Torches appear on the ridge above. A horn sounds, not one of theirs.*

> **ELDER:** *(distant, shouting)* To the terraces! Get the children to the terraces!

**RAID BEGINS.** *[Gameplay: forced-stealth sequence. The player, as the boy, must move from cover to cover toward the designated safe point. No fight prompts exist for Pillahuaso — the UI deliberately shows no attack option, only move/hide.]*

*Anta moves past Pillahuaso toward the fighting, shoving him toward a low stone wall.*

> **ANTA:** Stay down. Stay *here.* I mean it.

*Pillahuaso obeys — for a moment. Through gaps in the wall, we see Anta engage two raiders, holding a narrow path alone. He's good. He's also outnumbered.*

*A THIRD RAIDER approaches Anta's blind side. The game gives the player one prompt:*

*[QTE: "WARN HIM" — a single button prompt, deliberately generous timing window]*

*If the player succeeds: Anta still takes the hit — the QTE was never winnable, only the follow-through mattered. If the player fails or does nothing: same outcome, slightly faster. Either way—*

*Anta falls.*

> **PILLAHUASO:** *(barely audible)* No—

*He doesn't move. He wants to. His legs won't. The raiders pass within feet of him and don't see him — he is, for the first time, made invisible by his own fear, and it saves his life and costs him everything else.*

*[Gameplay ends here — hard cut, no further interaction this mission.]*

# M0.2 — "THE NAME THEY GAVE HIM"

**EXT. FOREST CLEARING — DAWN, DAYS LATER**

Smoke still rises faintly in the distance. WILLKA, 60s, kneels beside Pillahuaso, who sits wrapped in a blanket, staring at nothing.

> **WILLKA:** You've a name?

*(No answer.)*

> **WILLKA:** *(not pushing)* Fine. Keep it, then. Come on — up. Sitting in the dirt won't feed you and it won't bring anyone back.

**MONTAGE — SEVERAL WEEKS.** *[Gameplay: a series of short vignettes, lightly interactive — chopping wood, carrying water, first training-dummy strikes. Each vignette is brief, 30–90 seconds, stitched with time-skip transitions.]*

*In one vignette, other camp children mock Pillahuaso for not speaking. He says nothing — his face doesn't move at all, even as they push him. One boy, embarrassed by the lack of a reaction, backs off first.*

> **CAMP BOY:** *(to another kid, muttering as they walk off)* His face doesn't do anything. Like a rock.

> **CAMP BOY 2:** *(half-laughing)* Rumi... ñawi. Stone-face.

*The name spreads through the camp within days, half mockery, half something closer to respect. We see Pillahuaso hear it for the first time, alone, practicing strikes against a post.*

> **PILLAHUASO:** *(quiet, to himself, testing it)* Rumiñahui.

*He doesn't smile. But he doesn't correct the next person who says it, either.*

**EXT. TRAINING GROUND — LATER**

Willka watches him drill, arms crossed.

> **WILLKA:** You know why I don't call you by the name your mother gave you anymore?

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(still drilling)* Because you're old and forget things.

> **WILLKA:** *(dry)* Because you stopped answering to it. A name is worth what a person is willing to be, boy. You decided what you're willing to be a long time before I had any say in it.

*(Beat. Rumiñahui stops drilling.)*

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** I froze.

> **WILLKA:** You did.

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(flat)* I won't again.

> **WILLKA:** *(quietly)* I know. That's rather the problem with you, if I'm honest. But — later's problem. Again. From the top.

*[Gameplay: full combat tutorial resumes — light/heavy attack, dodge, block, taught over the remainder of the mission. Ends on the accepted name and the first real training bout, setting up M1.1.]*

# M5.5 — "SIGCHOS"

**EXT. HIGHLAND TRAIL, NEAR SIGCHOS — DAY, EARLY 1535**

RUMIÑAHUI, gaunt, older, moving alone through thin cover — Chaska and Atoc are elsewhere on this leg of the guerrilla campaign (established via brief dialogue at mission start; the split is deliberate, isolating him for what's coming).

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(to himself, checking a ridge line)* Two days to the meeting point. Less, if the pass is clear.

*[Gameplay: this mission mirrors M0.1's structure precisely — movement/stealth only, no attack prompts available, framed identically in UI to the Prologue so experienced players feel the echo immediately.]*

*He crosses open ground he shouldn't have. Spanish scouts — more than expected — close from two directions. This time, unlike the Prologue, he sees them coming.*

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(low, almost calm)* ...No.

*[QTE-style choice, framed honestly in the UI as unwinnable — no amount of input changes the outcome, only the manner of it: "STAND" or "RUN." Either choice leads to capture; "STAND" gives a slightly different, more dignified capture animation.]*

*He's taken. No dialogue as it happens — just the sound design and his face, which, true to the name, does not change.*

# M5.6 — "STONE FACE"

**INT. SPANISH HOLDING QUARTERS, NEAR QUITO — DAY(S) LATER**

Sparse, functional space. RUMIÑAHUI, injured, sits bound. A Spanish OFFICER (unnamed — deliberately kept generic per the story's choice not to build out individual Spanish characters) speaks through/with a QUECHUA-SPEAKING INTERPRETER.

> **INTERPRETER:** *(relaying, not translating word-for-word)* He wants to know where the gold went. Atahualpa's gold. He says it will go easier for you if you simply say it.

*(Rumiñahui says nothing. His face is entirely still.)*

> **INTERPRETER:** *(after a silence, quieter, almost apologetic)* He is going to hurt you. I think you understand that.

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(the only line he speaks in the scene, evenly)* Tell him my mother named me for the mountains. Tell him I already know what stone feels like.

*[Gameplay: the sequence that follows is deliberately NOT depicted directly. Hard cut to black on the line above. What the player experiences instead:]*

**INT. HOLDING QUARTERS — LATER (TIME UNSPECIFIED)**

*Return from black. Rumiñahui is alone, worse for it — visibly, but the camera stays wide and still; no close-up dwelling on injury. His breathing is heavy. His face, even now, is unreadable.*

*[Gameplay: a short, slow sequence — the only interaction is a repeated, quiet prompt: "STAY SILENT." It appears three times across the scene, spaced by stretches of stillness. There is no fail state; the prompt exists to give the player somewhere to put their hands, not to test them.]*

*After the third prompt, the Interpreter returns, alone this time.*

> **INTERPRETER:** *(quiet, no longer performing anything for anyone)* You could still tell me. Only me. I could tell them anything I wanted to.

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(a long beat — then, almost gently)* Then you'd be the one carrying it. I've carried enough for other people. Let this one be mine to keep.

*(The Interpreter has no answer to that. He leaves.)*

**EXT. EXECUTION GROUND, NEAR QUITO — DAY — JUNE 1535**

*A plain, unglorified space. No crowd shot for spectacle — a handful of Spanish soldiers, the Interpreter at a distance, watching, unable to do anything else.*

*Rumiñahui is brought forward. The camera holds on his face — the same stillness from the training post as a boy, the same stillness from the fireside with Willka.*

*No final speech. No last-second rescue. The scene cuts on the sound of wind through the highlands — the same sound design cue used in the Prologue's opening — before anything happens on screen, ending the mission on an implication rather than an image.*

# M5.7 — "WHAT THE MOUNTAINS KEEP" (EPILOGUE)

**EXT. LLANGANATES MOUNTAINS — SOME WEEKS LATER**

CHASKA, alone, climbs a ridge line. ATOC waits at the top, looking out over the valley toward a distant, smoke-thinned Quito.

> **ATOC:** You didn't need to come all the way up here to tell me.

> **CHASKA:** I didn't come to tell you. You already knew before I got here. I came because I didn't want to hear it alone.

*(A long silence. Wind. Atoc looks back toward the mountains behind them — the hidden treasure's direction, never shown directly on screen even now.)*

> **ATOC:** *(quiet)* Three of us knew where that gold went. Now it's two.

> **CHASKA:** Then it stays two. However long that takes.

> **ATOC:** *(almost a dry laugh, no real humor in it)* He'd have liked that. Something of his that even they can't touch.

> **CHASKA:** *(looking at the mountains, not at Atoc)* He'd have liked a lot of things he didn't get.

*[Gameplay: final short walking sequence — the player, as Chaska, walks the ridge alongside Atoc toward the tree line, no combat, no further dialogue prompts. They disappear into the trees together.]*

**TEXT CARD (closing the game):**

> *Rumiñahui was executed in June 1535, having never revealed the location of Atahualpa's treasure.*
>
> *To this day, the gold of the Inca has never been found in the Llanganates — and people are still looking.*

**[END]**

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Voice direction for Rumiñahui:** across every scene in this document, the character should be played with minimal vocal variation — the "stillness" is a performance choice as much as a writing one. Save any audible strain for breathing/physical performance, not line delivery.
- **The Interpreter** is deliberately the only named-in-dialogue Spanish-side voice in the entire ending. Recommend keeping him unnamed on screen/credits as "Interpreter" to preserve the story's choice not to individuate Spanish characters — the empire, not a villain, is the antagonist of Act V.
- **Torture restraint:** per the project's tone direction, nothing in M5.6 should be storyboarded with visible on-screen violence during the "missing time" cut. If playtesting wants more tension here, add sound design and lighting changes rather than added visuals.
