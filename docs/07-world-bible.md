# RUMIÑAHUI — World Bible & Art Direction
### Companion document to the Story Bible — visual/environmental reference for whoever builds environments

Text-based reference (no image generation here) — written so an art team, or an AI art-generation pass, has concrete direction rather than vague mood words alone.

---

## 1. OVERALL VISUAL PHILOSOPHY

- **Grounded over fantastical.** No glowing runes, no literal magic effects. Every "mythic" visual (Section 4) should look like it could be explained by firelight, exhaustion, or belief — never like an obvious special power.
- **Color as era-marker, not just mood.** The game's three broad visual periods (Andean forest/highland, Inca imperial, Spanish-conquest) should each read instantly by palette alone, even with the HUD off.
- **Scale used sparingly.** Reserve genuinely awe-inspiring vistas (wide Quito shots, the Llanganates peaks) for a handful of key moments so they don't get numbed out by overuse — GoW-style "camera stops to look" beats, not constant spectacle.

---

## 2. LOCATION: PÍLLARO & THE HIGHLAND FOREST (Prologue, Act I)

- **Terrain:** Cloud-forest edge meeting terraced highland agriculture — dense green canopy above, cultivated stepped fields below. Mist is a constant, low-lying, especially at dawn/dusk missions.
- **Palette:** Deep forest greens, wet-stone greys, warm firelight orange as the one consistent "safe" color against the cool, damp environment.
- **Architecture:** Simple stone-and-thatch village structures, communal in layout — no grand architecture yet; this place should feel intimate and small compared to what comes later, reinforcing Rumiñahui's marginal origin.
- **Key set-dressing beats:**
  - M0.1: the same village should look warm and lived-in in early scenes, then visually "wrong" the instant the raid starts — torchlight color shifting from warm orange to harsher, more clinical torch-orange-on-smoke.
  - M1.2 (fireside huaca): a single twisted, ancient tree and a modest stone circle — small-scale sacred, not monumental. The vision sequence here should use a desaturated, slightly overexposed look to separate it visually from "real" scenes without ever confirming what it is.

---

## 3. LOCATION: THE IMPERIAL WORLD (Acts II–IV)

- **Terrain:** Moves from frontier garrisons (rough, functional, still fairly plain) to Tumipampa/Quito's more developed imperial architecture as Rumiñahui rises in rank — the environment's polish should track his own rising status.
- **Palette:** Warmer, more saturated than Píllaro — golds, terracotta, deep reds in imperial spaces, reserved specifically for scenes involving Atahualpa and the royal court, so gold reads as "power" rather than as treasure-foreshadowing too early.
- **Architecture:** Iconic Inca stonework — precise, fitted, monumental masonry (echoing real Inca sites like Ingapirca, the northern-most major Inca ruin, genuinely in Cañar province near this story's region — worth referencing directly for authentic stonework style). Trapezoidal doorways and niches throughout as a consistent architectural signature.
- **Key set-dressing beats:**
  - M2.2 (military review): first appearance of genuine imperial scale — deliberately the game's first "camera stops to look" vista.
  - M3.5 (the huaca climax): should visually rhyme with M1.2's small fireside huaca, but scaled up and battle-damaged — same sacred-site visual language, now under siege.

---

## 4. LOCATION: QUITO — BEFORE AND AFTER THE FIRE (Act V)

This is the game's most important before/after visual contrast and deserves the most deliberate planning.

**Quito, intact (M5.1):**
- Full imperial palette from Section 3, at its most vivid — this should be the single most visually rich location in the entire game, specifically so its destruction lands harder.
- Temples and public plazas rendered with warmth and specificity — give the player something to recognize when it's gone.

**Quito, burning (M5.2):**
- Palette shift: golds and reds curdle into black smoke and orange fire-glow — same base hues as the intact city, corrupted rather than replaced, so the connection is visually legible.
- Recommend a hard framing rule: never show the fire as beautiful or triumphant. Lighting should feel harsh and unflattering — closer to disaster-footage color grading than cinematic "epic battle" grading.

**Quito, aftermath (referenced later, not necessarily revisited on-screen):**
- If shown again, should be grey, ash-toned, and empty — the golds from Section 3 should not reappear in this location again for the rest of the game.

---

## 5. LOCATION: THE LLANGANATES (Act V, M5.3 and beyond)

- **Terrain:** Real páramo highland/mountain terrain — cold, exposed, sparse vegetation compared to the lush lowland forest of Píllaro. Fog and low cloud cover should be near-constant, both for mood and because it's geographically accurate.
- **Palette:** Cold greys, muted greens, pale light — the game's least saturated location, intentionally, as a contrast to both the warmth of Píllaro and the gold of the imperial world. This is where the gold goes to be forgotten, visually as well as narratively.
- **Key set-dressing beats:**
  - M5.3's hiding chamber should be small, dark, and deliberately unglamorous — no treasure-room spectacle, reinforcing the design choice (see Mission List) that this moment isn't a reward beat.
  - M5.7 (epilogue): same terrain, but shot at a gentler time of day (recommend late afternoon light) — the only moment in Act V allowed a small amount of visual warmth back, signaling some peace without contradicting the ending's grief.

---

## 6. THE VISION SEQUENCES (cross-referenced with the Myth Ambiguity doc)

Recurring visual language for every Three-Worlds vision (M1.2, M3.5, and any others added later):
- Desaturated base palette, single accent color per vision (recommend a consistent pale gold-white for all of them, distinct from the "real world" gold of Section 3's imperial palette so it never gets confused with actual treasure/wealth imagery)
- Slight overexposure / soft-focus edges rather than any glowing/particle-effect magic language
- Condor/Puma/Amaru should be suggested through light, shadow, and brief glimpses — never a fully rendered, clearly-a-spirit-animal model. Ambiguity is the entire point (see Section 7 of the Myth Ambiguity doc).

---

## 7. CHARACTER VISUAL NOTES (brief — full model specs belong in a separate character art doc if needed later)

- **Rumiñahui:** should visually harden across the timeline — Prologue/Act I: soft-featured child/teen. Acts II–IV: increasingly scarred, more armor, visibly more controlled posture. Act V: gaunt, weathered, the "stone face" should read almost literally by this point — minimal expressiveness even in idle animation.
- **Chaska:** practical hunter/scout dress throughout, changing to captain-tier Inca military dress by Act III — recommend she keeps at least one visual element (a specific dyed textile pattern, a weapon shape) consistent from Prologue to Epilogue as an anchor point across the whole timeline.
- **Atoc:** should read as visually "other" during his antagonist stretch (Act III) — different regional dress/colors signaling Huáscar's faction — then gradually adopt shared visual elements with Rumiñahui's forces post-M3.6, tracking his loyalty shift visually rather than only through dialogue.
