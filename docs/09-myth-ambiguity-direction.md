# RUMIÑAHUI — Myth Ambiguity Direction
### How to signal "legend, not literal magic" to the player, in practical terms

The story bible's core rule — Andean cosmology is belief and vision, never confirmed literal power — only works if it's executed consistently at the level of camera, sound, and color, not just in the script. This doc gives concrete, repeatable direction so every vision sequence (M1.2, M3.5, and any added later) reads the same way without the writing needing to explain itself each time.

---

## 1. THE CENTRAL RULE

**Never let a vision sequence do anything a stressed, exhausted, grieving human mind couldn't plausibly produce on its own.** No object moves on its own. No enemy is affected by anything that isn't also explainable as the character's own physical action. The moment a vision visibly changes the physical world in a way ordinary action couldn't, the ambiguity is broken and it becomes literal magic — which the Story Bible explicitly avoids.

Practical test for any new vision beat: **"Could this exact shot be explained, without changing a single frame, as someone's exhausted mind laying an old story over something real that's already happening?"** If not, cut or rework it.

---

## 2. CAMERA

- **Vision sequences get a distinct camera language:** slightly handheld drift even in otherwise-locked cutscenes, subtle focus-breathing (soft pull in and out of focus), and marginally slower cuts than the surrounding real-world scenes.
- **Never a hard "magic activates" push-in.** Reserve fast push-ins for real, grounded emotional beats (e.g., Willka's death) so that visual language stays reserved for genuine stakes, not spectacle.
- **Return-to-reality cuts should be abrupt, not a fade.** A hard cut back to the real world (sound and light snapping back at once) reinforces that the vision was a mental event bounded in time, not a gradual scene transition.

---

## 3. SOUND

- **Vision sequences should never introduce a new "magic" sound palette (chimes, whooshes, choir stingers).** Instead, take existing diegetic sound (wind, fire crackle, Willka's actual recorded voice) and process it — slight reverb, slight pitch-down, subtle delay — so it's recognizably the *same world*, just heard differently under stress.
- **Willka's voice specifically** should be reused verbatim from his real recorded dialogue (flagged already in the M3.5 Flow Diagram's audio notes) rather than newly written "mystical" lines — this is the single most important consistency rule in the whole document, since it's the clearest tell to an attentive player that these moments are memory, not prophecy.
- **Silence is underused and valuable here.** A beat of near-total sound dropout before a vision begins reads as "something happening inside a person's head," where a swelling score reads as "something happening in the world."

---

## 4. COLOR & LIGHT

- Cross-referencing the World Bible (Section 6): desaturated base, single consistent pale gold-white accent, soft-focus edges, no particle effects.
- **The three spirit-animals (Puma/Kuntur/Amaru) should never be shown as complete, clearly-rendered creatures.** Suggest them through: a shadow shape that could be an animal or could be smoke/dust; a single eye-glint in darkness; a silhouette glimpsed only at the edge of frame, gone if the camera moves toward it. The player should be able to argue, after the credits roll, about whether they ever "really" appeared at all.
- **Consistency rule:** if a shape resembling one of the three animals ever appears outside a flagged vision sequence (e.g., ambient wildlife during a normal gameplay scene), it should be render exactly as a normal animal would — same lighting rules as everything else in that scene. Never let ordinary wildlife get a "meaningful" visual treatment; that cheapens the ambiguity everywhere else.

---

## 5. WRITING / DIALOGUE DISCIPLINE

- No character should ever say a flatly confirming line like "the spirits helped us" or a flatly denying line like "there was nothing there." The closest any character gets to addressing it directly is Willka's own established stance: *"it's a teaching, boy, not a truth."*
- Post-M3.5, Chaska and Rumiñahui's brief disagreement about "what happened" (Mission List, M3.6) should stay genuinely unresolved in the dialogue itself — recommend the two of them landing on different, equally plausible readings (exhaustion/adrenaline vs. something more) without either one winning the argument on-screen.

---

## 6. WHERE THIS RULE BENDS (the one exception, and why it's allowed)

The ending's text card (Dialogue Script, M5.7) states plainly that the Llanganates treasure has never been found in real life. This is **not** a myth-ambiguity moment — it's a factual historical/legendary note about the real world, clearly marked as a text card rather than a vision sequence, and should stay visually and tonally distinct from every in-fiction vision beat in the game. Keep these two kinds of "the story becomes legend" clearly separated: one is a character's private, ambiguous experience; the other is the real world's own unresolved history.
