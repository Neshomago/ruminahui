# RUMIÑAHUI — Mission / Level List
### Companion document to the Story Bible — maps every act to playable content

Format per mission: **Playable character(s)** · **Location** · **Narrative beat** · **Gameplay notes**

---

## PROLOGUE — "The Boy Who Froze" (Píllaro, c. 1500)

### M0.1 — "Night Raid"
- **Plays as:** Pillahuaso (age 10)
- **Location:** Píllaro village, night
- **Beat:** Establish the village, the family, the ordinary evening — then the raid hits. The boy hides. A failed QTE. Someone he loves dies defending the home.
- **Gameplay:** No combat abilities. Stealth/movement only, one desperate failed prompt. Deliberately disempowering — this is the emotional baseline everything else answers.

### M0.2 — "The Name They Gave Him"
- **Plays as:** Pillahuaso (age 10, weeks later)
- **Location:** Willka's camp, forest outskirts
- **Beat:** Aftermath. Willka takes the boy in. Time-skip montage of the boy training his face into stillness. Other children start calling him Rumiñahui.
- **Gameplay:** Short, mostly cinematic with light training-dummy combat tutorial (basic attack/dodge). Ends on the boy accepting the name.

## ACT I — "Stone Is Made, Not Born" (age 10–18)

### M1.1 — "Two Prodigies"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui (teen)
- **Location:** Píllaro highlands, training grounds
- **Beat:** Introduce Chaska as the faster, sharper rival/equal. First real combat tutorial framed as a training duel he loses.
- **Gameplay:** Full combat tutorial (light/heavy attacks, dodge, parry) taught through losing to a superior opponent — humility built into the mechanics lesson.

### M1.2 — "The Three Worlds"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui (teen)
- **Location:** Fireside huaca
- **Beat:** Willka's telling of the Puma/Condor/Amaru story. First vision sequence — stylized, ambiguous, non-combat.
- **Gameplay:** Exploration/vision level, puzzle-lite, no combat. Establishes the visual language for later vision sequences.

### M1.3 — "What Discipline Buys"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui (18)
- **Location:** Border skirmish near Píllaro
- **Beat:** A raid on the region. Rumiñahui's disciplined approach (formation, patience) wins where raw aggression fails. A visiting Inca recruiter takes notice.
- **Gameplay:** First "real" combat encounter against multiple enemies — teaches crowd-control fundamentals (Puma kit basics).

### M1.4 — "Leaving the Forest" *(short transitional/cinematic level)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Píllaro → the road south
- **Beat:** Farewell to Chaska (not forever — plant the seed she'll re-enter later) and Willka's decision to follow him. Establishes the world scale beyond the village.
- **Gameplay:** Walking/dialogue level, minimal combat. Tone-setting before the time skip.

## ACT II — "The General's Apprentice" (age ~18–28)

### M2.1 — "Garrison Duty"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui (young soldier)
- **Location:** Frontier garrison
- **Beat:** Fast-forward montage structure — several small campaigns compressed. Establish Rumiñahui's reputation rising through merit, not birth.
- **Gameplay:** Escalating combat gauntlet across 3 short sub-encounters; introduces enemy variety (skirmishers, shield-bearers).

### M2.2 — "The Prince Notices"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Military review, near Tumipampa or Quito
- **Beat:** First real meeting with Atahualpa. Political texture: courtiers' resentment of the "marginal prince." Atahualpa's private respect established.
- **Gameplay:** Mostly dialogue/cinematic; one guarded-escort combat sequence (protect a convoy).

### M2.3 — "Willka's Last Stand"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Ambush on a mountain pass
- **Beat:** Rumiñahui's overconfidence nearly gets him killed; Willka steps in and dies protecting him. Emotional low point of the act.
- **Gameplay:** A hard, humbling combat encounter that the player is meant to struggle with, followed by a scripted, uninteractive loss (Willka's death) — deliberately takes control away from the player at the worst moment.

### M2.4 — "The Empire Splits"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Army camp, receiving the messenger
- **Beat:** News of Huayna Cápac's death. The succession crisis. Atahualpa gives Rumiñahui a general's command.
- **Gameplay:** Cinematic-heavy "promotion" level; light command-tutorial (introduces any squad/ally-command mechanics that will matter in Act III).

## ACT III — "The Long Battle" (Civil War climax — the campaign's centerpiece)

### M3.1 — "Opening Moves"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Northern approach to Huáscar's territory
- **Beat:** First large-scale battle as general. Reintroduce Chaska, now a captain in her own right.
- **Gameplay:** Large-scale battlefield combat with squad-command elements; Chaska fights as an AI ally with unique moves (foreshadowing her playable sections).

### M3.2 — "The Fox's Ground" *(playable as Chaska)*
- **Plays as:** Chaska
- **Location:** Forward scouting position near Mullihambato
- **Beat:** A scouting mission that reveals Atoc's tactical brilliance firsthand — establishing him as a genuine threat before the player fights him as Rumiñahui.
- **Gameplay:** Stealth/speed-focused level, introduces Condor-style verticality and ranged mechanics early via Chaska's perspective.

### M3.3 — "Siege of Mullihambato, Part I"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Mullihambato battlefield
- **Beat:** The siege proper begins. Escalating multi-wave combat. First direct clash with Atoc's forces.
- **Gameplay:** Large set-piece battle; mini-boss encounter against one of Atoc's lieutenants.

### M3.4 — "The Mercy" *(boss encounter)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Mullihambato battlefield, the breach point
- **Beat:** Direct duel against Atoc. Rumiñahui wins and — against Atahualpa's standing orders — spares and captures him instead of executing him. Real political cost flagged here for later payoff.
- **Gameplay:** The game's first true boss fight; skill-based one-on-one duel with a defined "spare" prompt at the finishing blow instead of the expected kill prompt.

### M3.5 — "What Held the Line" *(the climax — three-character sequence)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui → Chaska → Atoc (perspective shifts mid-mission)
- **Location:** The collapsing position at the huaca
- **Beat:** The centerpiece "long battle." The position is failing; exhaustion and blood loss bring on the shared trance-vision. Each character, in turn, becomes what the old story described — puma, condor, serpent — to hold the line.
- **Gameplay:** The mission's structure IS the power-sharing payoff: the player literally plays all three combat styles back-to-back-to-back in one unbroken sequence, each unlocked mid-mission rather than via a menu. This is the game's signature set-piece.

### M3.6 — "After the Fire" *(quiet cooldown level)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Camp, day after the battle
- **Beat:** Aftermath. Atoc, still a prisoner, is offered a place rather than a cell. Chaska and Rumiñahui process what happened at the huaca without ever fully agreeing on what it was.
- **Gameplay:** No combat. Dialogue/exploration level; full unlock of the Three-Worlds ability menu going forward.

## ACT IV — "The Emperor's Fall" (1532–1533)

### M4.1 — "Strange Ships"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Northern camp, receiving reports
- **Beat:** Atahualpa's coronation, brief and triumphant, immediately undercut by reports of foreign ships and armed strangers inland.
- **Gameplay:** Short cinematic-forward level with one skirmish against an unrelated local threat — keeps the pacing from going fully static before the gut-punch.

### M4.2 — "The News from Cajamarca"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Camp, far from Cajamarca
- **Beat:** The capture of Atahualpa arrives as a messenger's report — deliberately not shown directly. Rumiñahui's fury at not being there.
- **Gameplay:** A rage-fueled optional combat gauntlet (venting at nothing — training dummies, wildlife, whatever the world offers) as a mechanical expression of grief, entirely skippable for players who don't want it.

### M4.3 — "The Execution" / "North to Quito"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Camp → the road to Quito
- **Beat:** Confirmation of Atahualpa's execution. Rumiñahui, Chaska, and Atoc decide to turn north — no longer imperial soldiers, now the last defenders.
- **Gameplay:** Walking/dialogue level closing the act; sets tone for the darker final act.

---

## ACT V — "Ashes and the Mountain's Secret" (1534–1535) — FINAL ACT

### M5.1 — "Benalcázar's Advance"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Approaches to Quito
- **Beat:** First contact with Spanish forces. Establish Benalcázar as a distant, encroaching threat rather than a personal rival — this isn't his story.
- **Gameplay:** New enemy type introduced (Spanish infantry/cavalry) with different combat rhythm than Andean opponents — mechanically signals the stakes have changed.

### M5.2 — "The Hardest Order"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Quito, under siege
- **Beat:** The decision point: evacuate, deal with collaborators, and burn the city rather than let it fall intact. Framed as a genuine moral weight, not a triumphant "epic villain" moment.
- **Gameplay:** A mission built around escort/evacuation objectives under time pressure, culminating in a scripted, deliberately uncomfortable "light the fire" beat — the game should not make this feel good to do.

### M5.3 — "Into the Llanganates" *(three-character mission)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui, Chaska, Atoc (player can likely switch freely)
- **Location:** Llanganates mountains
- **Beat:** The three of them, alone, move Atahualpa's treasure into hiding. Deliberately small and quiet after M5.2's city-scale destruction.
- **Gameplay:** Traversal-heavy level using all three movement kits (Puma climbing/strength obstacles, Condor gliding/height, Amaru narrow-gap/trap-sense navigation) — a cooperative puzzle-platforming showcase before the game turns grim again.

### M5.4 — "The Long Retreat"
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui (with Chaska/Atoc as AI allies)
- **Location:** Highland guerrilla campaign, multiple sub-locations
- **Beat:** Montage-style guerrilla war against Benalcázar's forces — hit and run, dwindling resources, mounting exhaustion.
- **Gameplay:** A run of shorter, harder ambush-style encounters with limited healing — mechanically communicating attrition.

### M5.5 — "Sigchos" *(capture — the game's second powerless sequence, mirroring the Prologue)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Sigchos
- **Beat:** Captured. Deliberately structured to echo the Prologue's powerlessness — bookending the story on the same emotional note it began with, except this time he doesn't freeze.
- **Gameplay:** No combat abilities available; tense stealth/evasion attempt that is meant to fail no matter what the player does — an intentional, telegraphed "unwinnable" sequence.

### M5.6 — "Stone Face" *(finale)*
- **Plays as:** Rumiñahui
- **Location:** Spanish custody, near Quito
- **Beat:** Torture depicted through implication and aftermath, never graphically — his silence, his stillness, the name he earned as a boy now literally the only thing holding. He never gives up the treasure's location. Execution, June 1535.
- **Gameplay:** A restrained, mostly non-interactive final sequence — a handful of meaningful prompts (stay silent / stay silent / stay silent) rather than any combat, ending on his death.

### M5.7 — "What the Mountains Keep" *(epilogue)*
- **Plays as:** Chaska (final playable scene)
- **Location:** Llanganates, some time later
- **Beat:** Chaska and Atoc, the last two who know, choose to vanish into the mountains rather than return to a Spanish-held Quito. Closing text card bridges into the real, ongoing Llanganates treasure legend.
- **Gameplay:** Short, non-combat walking finale. Ends the game on quiet rather than spectacle.

## STRUCTURAL SUMMARY

| Act | Missions | Playable focus |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 2 | Rumiñahui (child) |
| Act I | 4 | Rumiñahui (teen) |
| Act II | 4 | Rumiñahui (young adult) |
| Act III | 6 | Rumiñahui, Chaska, Atoc |
| Act IV | 3 | Rumiñahui |
| Act V | 7 | Rumiñahui, Chaska, Atoc |
| **Total** | **26 missions** | |

This gives a full campaign roughly in line with a AAA action-adventure of this type (comparable in scope to *God of War*'s chapter count), with three clear tentpole set-pieces to prototype first: **M3.5** (the power-sharing climax), **M5.2** (the burning of Quito), and **M5.6** (the execution finale).
