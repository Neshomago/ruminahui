# RUMIÑAHUI — "Face of Stone"
### Story Bible & Narrative Script Treatment for an Action-Adventure Game

---

## 1. LOGLINE

Born a prince too marginal to inherit, Pillahuaso earns a warrior's name in blood — Rumiñahui, "Stone Face" — and rises to become the general who nearly saved an empire, then the last man who refused to let it be robbed of everything, even as it cost him his life.

**Setting:** Píllaro and the northern Inca Empire (modern Ecuador), 1490–1535. The story spans from Rumiñahui's birth through the Inca Civil War to the Spanish conquest and his execution — a complete life, structured as one full game.

**Tone:** Grounded and serious. Violence is consequential and felt, not gratuitous. Mythology appears as vision, memory, and belief — never as literal gods intervening. The one exception the story earns is the ending: history itself becomes legend, in the very real, still-told story of Inca gold hidden in the Llanganates.

---

## 2. THE MYTHOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK (grounded, not literal)

Real Andean cosmology, treated as lived belief rather than active magic — seen in visions, trance, grief, and exhaustion, but never confirmed by the story as literally real.

**The Three Worlds (Quechua cosmology):**
- **Hanan Pacha** — the world above; sky, sun, ancestors. Symbol: the **Kuntur (Condor)**.
- **Kay Pacha** — the world here; earth, the living, struggle. Symbol: the **Puma**.
- **Uku Pacha** — the world below; roots, the dead, transformation. Symbol: the **Amaru (Serpent)**.

The oldest teaching story of the Andes: every person carries all three worlds inside them — the vigilance of the condor, the strength of the puma, the cunning and rebirth of the serpent. A warrior who has mastered all three is said to "walk whole between the worlds." This becomes the game's ability framework (Section 6).

**Huacas** — sacred sites (springs, twisted trees, stone outcrops) function as the shrine/checkpoint/vision-sequence system throughout.

## 3. MAIN CHARACTERS

### Rumiñahui, born Pillahuaso — Protagonist
- **Born:** Píllaro, 1490. **Died:** executed near Quito, June 1535.
- **Parentage:** A son of the sovereign Huayna Cápac and a local princess of Píllaro — royal blood, but not from a principal wife, which leaves him with rank but no real claim to succession. He grows up inside the court's orbit and outside its trust at the same time: enough royal blood to be resented, not enough to be safe.
- **Birth name:** Pillahuaso. *Rumiñahui* ("stone eye/face," from *rumi* = stone, *ñawi* = eye/face) is a name he earns, not one he's born with — tied directly to the story's core wound (see Prologue).
- **Core wound:** As a boy in Píllaro, he freezes during a raid and someone he loves dies because of it. He teaches himself, afterward, to never let his face show fear again — and it's that deliberate, learned stillness that later earns him the name.
- **Arc:** Frightened boy → marginal prince who has to out-work everyone → battlefield prodigy → general → the last loyal man of a fallen empire → a legend who died with a secret rather than give it up.
- **Embodies:** **Puma** — endurance, weight, unbreakable ground-based combat.

### Chaska ("Star") — The Ally
- A hunter and scout from the Píllaro highlands, sharper and faster than Rumiñahui from childhood. Written as a fierce equal, not a subordinate.
- **Arc:** Reckless prodigy → tempered captain after the cost of the Long Battle (Act III) → one of only three people alive who will know where Atahualpa's treasure is hidden (Act V), and one of only two who survive to carry that secret into legend.
- **Embodies:** **Kuntur (Condor)** — speed, verticality, ranged/aerial combat.

### Atoc ("Fox") — The Rival-Turned-Ally
- Historically real: a general for Huáscar's faction, defeated near Mullihambato/Ambato. Built here as Rumiñahui's dark mirror — low birth, same hunger, chose the other brother.
- **Arc:** Enemy general → captured and spared by Rumiñahui at real political cost → earns trust across Act III → by Act V, one of the only people Rumiñahui trusts enough to bring into the Llanganates.
- **Embodies:** **Amaru (Serpent)** — cunning, traps, terrain, counter-ambush.

### Willka — The Mentor
- An aging amauta (warrior-priest) who takes the boy in after the Píllaro tragedy. Teaches him to fight and teaches him the Three Worlds story — always insisting "it's a teaching, boy, not a truth." Dies in battle partway through Act II.

### Atahualpa — Rumiñahui's Half-Brother and Lord
- Historically real. Intelligent, charismatic, genuinely fond of Rumiñahui — and also a sovereign who will spend his general's life without hesitation when the empire requires it. His capture and execution at Cajamarca in 1533 is the hinge that turns the story from a civil war epic into a resistance story.

### Huáscar — The Distant Antagonist (Acts II–III)
- Kept largely offstage; his generals, especially Atoc, are the ones players actually fight. Keeps the focus on Rumiñahui rather than a two-emperor drama.

### Sebastián de Benalcázar — The Final Antagonist (Act V)
- Historically real Spanish conquistador who advanced on Quito in 1534. Deliberately kept as a distant, encroaching force for most of Act V — the story is about Rumiñahui's choices, not a Spanish character study.

## 4. STRUCTURE

### PROLOGUE — "The Boy Who Froze" (Píllaro, ~1500)
*(Playable flashback, ~15–20 min)*
- Pillahuaso, age 10, son of a princess of Píllaro, aware from an early age that his royal blood buys him resentment more than protection.
- A raid on Píllaro at night. The boy hides, freezes, and is unable to act while someone close to him is killed defending the village.
- Willka finds him afterward and takes him in. In the following weeks, the boy is seen practicing alone, forcing his face to stay unreadable no matter what — the other children start calling him *Rumiñahui*, "stone face," half-mocking, half-afraid of him. He keeps the name.
- **Gameplay hook:** intentionally near-powerless — movement, hiding, one failed QTE attempt to help. The player feels the helplessness that defines the character and names him.

### ACT I — "Stone Is Made, Not Born" (age 10–18)
- Brutal, unglamorous training under Willka. Introduce Chaska as a village equal who consistently outperforms him.
- Willka's fireside telling of the Three Worlds story — the game's first vision sequence, deliberately ambiguous (real, or a child's imagination?).
- Act closer: Rumiñahui is noticed by a military recruiter after a skirmish won through discipline, not talent. He leaves Píllaro for the army — and for the first time, his royal blood is an asset instead of a liability.

### ACT II — "The General's Apprentice" (age ~18–28)
- Escalating campaigns under the empire's army. Rumiñahui meets Atahualpa properly — the prince recognizes something in this marginal half-brother that his own inner circle lacks: someone with everything to prove and no faction of his own to be loyal to but the army itself.
- Willka's death in a mid-act battle, protecting Rumiñahui from a mistake born of overconfidence — the emotional low point that commits Rumiñahui fully to discipline over glory.
- Huayna Cápac dies (offscreen/by messenger). The empire splits between Huáscar and Atahualpa. Atahualpa gives Rumiñahui a general's command — precisely because his marginal birth means his only loyalty can be to Atahualpa personally, not to a rival house.

### ACT III — "The Long Battle" (the Civil War climax)
- A sustained, multi-mission siege campaign against Huáscar's northern armies, culminating near Mullihambato (Ambato region).
- Chaska fights alongside Rumiñahui as her own captain; Atoc leads the opposing force.
- Turning point: Rumiñahui captures Atoc rather than executing him — against Atahualpa's explicit wishes, a real political cost that pays off in Act V.
- Climax: at a huaca on the collapsing battlefield, exhausted past reason, Rumiñahui, Chaska, and the still-prisoner Atoc (fighting beside them out of pure survival instinct) hold the line together. In the middle of it, a shared trance — brought on by exhaustion and Willka's old story — in which each of them becomes, for one desperate stretch, what the story described: the puma that won't yield, the condor that sees the gap, the serpent that turns a losing position into a trap. Whether it was real or three exhausted minds converging on the same old lesson is left open.
- The battle is won. Atoc, spared and now proven, is given a place in Rumiñahui's forces — not trusted yet, but earning it.

### ACT IV — "The Emperor's Fall" (1532–1533)
- Atahualpa wins the civil war and is crowned — briefly. Word arrives of strange ships on the coast: Francisco Pizarro's expedition.
- Rumiñahui is not present at Cajamarca (historically accurate) — the player experiences Atahualpa's capture and eventual execution (1533) as devastating news delivered to camp, not as a playable Cajamarca level. This is a deliberate structural choice: the story stays inside Rumiñahui's perspective and his grief/rage at not being there.
- Act closer: Rumiñahui, Chaska, and Atoc turn north for Quito, no longer soldiers of an empire but the last defenders of one.

### ACT V — "Ashes and the Mountain's Secret" (1534–1535) — FINAL ACT
- Rumiñahui organizes Quito's defense against the advancing Sebastián de Benalcázar (1534).
- Facing certain defeat, he makes the story's hardest choice: orders the evacuation of Quito, has some allies who would collaborate with the Spanish put to death, and burns the city and its temples rather than let Benalcázar take it intact — a scorched-earth decision the game should let the player feel the full moral weight of, not glorify.
- With Chaska and Atoc — and no one else — he moves Atahualpa's treasure into the Llanganates mountains and hides it. This is deliberately staged as a small, quiet, three-person mission after an act of city-scale destruction: the personal against the historical.
- A guerrilla campaign follows: hit-and-run resistance against Benalcázar's forces through the highlands.
- Early 1535: captured near Sigchos. Imprisoned and tortured by the Spanish for the treasure's location. Per the story's restrained tone, this is depicted through implication and aftermath — his silence, his ruined body, his unreadable stone face — rather than graphic on-screen torture. He never talks.
- June 1535: executed.
- **Epilogue:** Chaska and Atoc, the only other two who know where the gold is, disappear into the mountains rather than ever return to a Spanish-controlled Quito. A closing text card can bridge into the real world: the Llanganates treasure has never been found, and search expeditions continue into the present day — turning the game's fiction directly into the region's living legend.

## 5. SAMPLE SCENE (tone reference)

**INT. FOREST CAMP — NIGHT — ACT I**

Firelight. WILLKA (60s, weathered, missing two fingers on his left hand) turns a spit of roasted maize. RUMIÑAHUI (14) sits across from him, arms wrapped around his knees.

> **WILLKA:** They say a person is three things wearing one skin. Up there — *(points skyward)* — the condor, who sees what's coming before it arrives. Here — *(taps the ground)* — the puma, who does not run from what arrives anyway. And below — *(taps his own chest)* — the serpent, who is killed a hundred times and comes back up out of the dirt a hundred and one.

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** Which one are you?

> **WILLKA:** *(a dry laugh)* An old man who talks too much by the fire. That's not an answer, boy. It's one you find out, usually on the worst day of your life.

> **RUMIÑAHUI:** *(quiet)* I already know which one I am.

> **WILLKA:** *(not unkindly)* No. You know which one you were, once, at ten years old. That is not the same thing as which one you are.

*Rumiñahui says nothing. The fire pops. Willka goes back to turning the maize.*

## 6. GAMEPLAY / NARRATIVE POWER SYSTEM

| Spirit | Character | Combat Style | Unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puma (Kay Pacha) | Rumiñahui | Heavy stance, blocking/counters, crowd control | Baseline + deepened after Act III climax |
| Kuntur (Hanan Pacha) | Chaska | Speed, verticality, ranged (sling/bolas), aerial finishers | Playable flashback + co-op sections |
| Amaru (Uku Pacha) | Atoc | Traps, terrain manipulation, herbal/toxin debuffs, counter-ambush | Unlocked as an ally-assist system after his redemption lands |

Suggestion: give the player brief **playable sections as Chaska and Atoc**, especially in Act III and Act V's Llanganates sequence, so the "power sharing" pays off mechanically as well as narratively.

## 7. HISTORICAL NOTE & CREATIVE LIBERTIES

Rumiñahui, Atahualpa, Huáscar, Atoc, and Sebastián de Benalcázar are real historical figures. The overall shape of this treatment — birth in Píllaro (1490), parentage as a son of Huayna Cápac by a local princess, the birth name Pillahuaso, his role in the civil war, the burning of Quito, the hiding of treasure in the Llanganates, his capture near Sigchos, torture, and execution in June 1535 — follows the traditional Ecuadorian historical account, as summarized in the brief you provided.

Worth flagging for transparency: some of these details (especially his exact parentage and the treasure legend) come from tradition and popular historiography rather than uncontested academic consensus — some historians describe him as of non-royal or lower origin who rose through merit rather than as Atahualpa's half-brother. Since this is being treated as the game's canon per your direction, that's a fine creative choice — just worth knowing if you want to add a soft in-game disclaimer ("based on tradition and historical accounts") given Rumiñahui's real weight as a national hero in Ecuador.

Continued deliberate liberties for the character drama:
- **Chaska is an invented character** — no historical record of a specific woman in this role.
- **Willka is an invented mentor** — a composite of the amauta role, not a specific historical person.
- **Atoc's fate is changed** — historically captured and killed, with later (disputed) accounts of brutal treatment of his remains; here he's spared and redeemed per your earlier direction, and lives into Act V.
- The Píllaro childhood tragedy, the origin of the nickname, specific battle staging, and the vision sequences are all fictionalized for dramatic structure.
